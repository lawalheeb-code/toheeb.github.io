E-Commerce Customer Behavior, Product Performance & Sales Analysis

A multi-table SQL analysis exploring customer interactions, product performance, purchasing behavior, revenue, reviews, and user characteristics to identify patterns and business opportunities.

View SQL Analysis View Business Insights
01 — Project Overview

From raw e-commerce data to business insights

The objective of this project was to use SQL to investigate how customers interact with products, how products perform, how sales are distributed, and what customer and review patterns can be observed across the e-commerce dataset.

Business Questions

  • How are customers interacting with products?
  • Which products and categories receive the most attention?
  • Which products generate the most purchases and revenue?
  • What does purchasing behavior look like across users?
  • What patterns exist in customer reviews and ratings?
  • Are there products receiving many views but relatively few purchases?

Duplicate Investigation

Data quality 
WITH duplicate_check AS (
    SELECT
        interaction_id,
        user_id,
        product_id,
        interaction_type,
        timestamp,
        dwell_time_ms,

        ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
            PARTITION BY
                user_id,
                product_id,
                interaction_type,
                timestamp,
                dwell_time_ms
            ORDER BY interaction_id
        ) AS row_num

    FROM interactions2
)

SELECT *
FROM duplicate_check
WHERE row_num > 1;

Product Views vs Purchases

Product performance screening 
SELECT
    p.product_name,
    COUNT(DISTINCT CASE
        WHEN i.interaction_type = 'view'
        THEN i.user_id
    END) AS unique_viewers,

    COUNT(DISTINCT pu.purchase_id) AS purchases

FROM products2 p

LEFT JOIN interactions2 i
    ON p.product_id = i.product_id

LEFT JOIN purchases2 pu
    ON p.product_id = pu.product_id

GROUP BY
    p.product_id,
    p.product_name

ORDER BY
    unique_viewers DESC;

Average Order Value

Sales analysis 
SELECT
    ROUND(
        SUM(total_amount) / COUNT(DISTINCT order_id),
        2
    ) AS average_order_value

FROM purchases2;

Average Product Rating

Customer reviews 
SELECT
    ROUND(AVG(rating), 2) AS average_rating

FROM reviews2;
Analytical note: The views-versus-purchases query is used as a screening analysis to identify products receiving attention without equivalent purchasing activity. It should not be interpreted as a true conversion rate unless the viewer and purchase populations are defined consistently.

09 — Business Insights

What the analysis could mean for the business

The purpose of the analysis was not simply to produce SQL results, but to translate those results into questions and actions a business team could investigate further.

01

Investigate high-view, low-purchase products

Products receiving significant attention but relatively few purchases may have issues related to pricing, product positioning, reviews, availability or checkout friction.

02

Improve conversion analysis

View-to-purchase behavior can be investigated further by calculating conversion at the unique user level and segmenting by category, product, device and customer type.

03

Monitor top-performing products

Products contributing strongly to both purchases and revenue should be monitored for inventory, pricing and promotional opportunities.

04

Treat ratings in context

Product ratings should be considered alongside review volume. High ratings based on very few reviews may not represent reliable customer sentiment.

05

Segment customer behavior

Customer demographics and membership tiers can be combined with purchasing behavior to identify valuable segments and opportunities for targeted campaigns.

10 — Data Quality

Data cleaning & validation

Before analysis, I performed validation and cleaning steps to improve consistency and reduce the risk of misleading results.

Duplicate checks

Used ROW_NUMBER() with business-relevant columns to identify potential duplicate interaction records.

Text standardization

Used TRIM and REPLACE where necessary to remove unnecessary whitespace and standardize text values.

Missing values

Checked NULL values and used appropriate SQL functions such as COALESCE and NULLIF where required.

Row-count validation

Compared table sizes and query outputs throughout the workflow to ensure transformations did not unintentionally alter the dataset.

11 — Limitations & Next Steps

What I would investigate next

The analysis provides descriptive insights, but several areas could be developed further with additional analysis.

True conversion rate

Calculate user-level conversion from product view to purchase rather than comparing raw interaction and purchase counts.

Customer segmentation

Build RFM-style customer segments using recency, frequency and monetary value.

Cohort analysis

Investigate how customer behavior changes after their first purchase.

Statistical relationships

Test relationships between price, ratings, engagement and purchasing behavior rather than relying only on descriptive comparisons.

This project focused primarily on descriptive and diagnostic analysis using SQL. The next stage would be to combine the SQL output with visualization and deeper statistical analysis to communicate trends more effectively.
